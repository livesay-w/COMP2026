# Rediscovering the nuclear mass formula with symbolic regression

Given only the measured binding energies of ~2,500 nuclei, can symbolic regression (PySR) rediscover the
semi-empirical (Bethe–Weizsäcker) mass formula, and what does each step up in formula complexity buy or cost?

B(Z, N) = a_V A − a_S A^{2/3} − a_C Z²/A^{1/3} − a_A (N − Z)²/A ± δ,   A = Z + N

| Term | Physics | Rough size |
|---|---|---|
| volume a_V A | each nucleon binds to its neighbours | a_V ≈ 15.8 MeV |
| surface −a_S A^{2/3} | surface nucleons have fewer neighbours | a_S ≈ 18 MeV |
| Coulomb −a_C Z²/A^{1/3} | protons repel | a_C ≈ 0.7 MeV |
| asymmetry −a_A (N − Z)²/A | the Pauli principle favours N ≈ Z | a_A ≈ 23 MeV |
| pairing ± δ | even–even more bound, odd–odd less | δ ≈ 12/√A MeV |

Data: AME2020 (`data/mass_1.mas20.txt`, from https://www-nds.iaea.org/amdc/ame2020/mass_1.mas20.txt;
Huang et al. and Wang et al., Chinese Physics C 45, 030002–3 (2021)). Energies in MeV.

Interpreter: miniconda base, `C:\Users\wrl62\miniconda3\python.exe` (PySR 2.7.2, Julia 1.13.4). PySR runs are
seeded and deterministic (`random_state=0, deterministic=True, parallelism="serial"`).
